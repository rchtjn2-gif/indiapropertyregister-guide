# Khasra, khatauni, 7/12, RTC: India's land-record names decoded

Land is a state subject in India, so every state keeps its own land records, in its own portal, with its own names. The same idea, a record of who holds which plot, can be called a khatauni, a 7/12 extract, an RTC or a patta depending on where you are.

## Common names and what they usually mean

| Term | Where you will hear it | What it generally is |
|---|---|---|
| Khasra (number) | North India (UP, MP, Rajasthan, Haryana and others) | The plot or survey number, and the register of plots |
| Khatauni | Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and nearby | Record of holders (khata-wise) and their plots |
| Khata / khewat | Several states | Holding or account number grouping a holder's land |
| Jamabandi | Punjab, Haryana, Himachal, Rajasthan | Record of rights showing owners and cultivators |
| 7/12 (Satbara) | Maharashtra | Extract combining forms VII (rights) and XII (cultivation) |
| Gat number | Maharashtra | Consolidated plot number |
| RTC (Pahani) | Karnataka | Record of Rights, Tenancy and Crops |
| Patta / chitta | Tamil Nadu | Patta shows ownership; chitta shows land details |

Names and formats vary even within states, so treat this as a starting point.

## Record of rights vs title

A record of rights (khatauni, 7/12, RTC and so on) shows who is recorded as holding the land for revenue purposes. It is strong evidence, but it is **not** by itself proof of title. Title comes from the chain of registered documents (sale deeds, partition, inheritance) and is checked through a title search.

## Finding a village and plot

[India Property Register](https://indiapropertyregister.com/) places each source on the same spine of Local Government Directory (LGD) codes and plot numbers. Start at the [India Property Registry](https://indiapropertyregister.com/ipregistry), pick a state, district, tehsil and village, then search by khasra, survey, gat or khata number.

## When you need a certified copy and an advocate

A page on any website, including this register, is not a certified copy or proof of title. For a sale, loan, court case or dispute, get certified copies from the state's revenue office or portal and have an advocate do a title search.
