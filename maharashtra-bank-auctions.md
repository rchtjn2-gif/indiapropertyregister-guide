# Bank auction properties in Maharashtra: a buyer's guide

Maharashtra is the second-busiest state for bank auctions on [India Property Register](https://indiapropertyregister.com/), after Haryana. The register shows **15,689 Maharashtra listings** out of **1,23,313** across 35 states and union territories (October 2026). Listings run from small 1 BHK flats in Raigad and Nashik with reserve prices in the low lakhs, through offices and flats in Mumbai Suburban reserved at several crore, to industrial plots with factory buildings in Raigad and liquidation sales of whole companies.

This guide explains how to read those listings and what to check before you bid. It is written by the India Property Register team. The register is built from public records and is not a certified copy or proof of title.

## Three kinds of sale in one list

The [Maharashtra bank auctions page](https://indiapropertyregister.com/auctions/maharashtra) mixes three different kinds of sale. They follow different rules, so identify which one you are looking at first.

| Type | How it appears on the register | Who sells | What governs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| SARFAESI bank sale | "2 BHK Flat for sale in …", with a bank and branch | The secured lender (bank) | The bank's sale notice under the SARFAESI Act |
| DRT recovery sale | "RC No. …/2023 … Date of Auction Sale …", seller shown as a Debts Recovery Tribunal | A DRT recovery officer | The tribunal's sale proclamation |
| Liquidation auction | "Liquidation auction: [company name]", seller shown as a named individual | The liquidator of a company | Insolvency and liquidation process |

On a recent first page of Maharashtra listings (50 rows), SARFAESI bank sales made up most entries, there were **seven liquidation auctions** run by named liquidators in Palghar, Thane and Pune, and one DRT Pune recovery sale.

### SARFAESI bank sales

These are the most common. A bank has taken possession after a borrower defaulted and sells the property to recover the loan. On that same first page, **Canara Bank** and **Bank of Baroda** listed the most properties, followed by State Bank of India, Punjab National Bank, Indian Bank and Central Bank of India.

### DRT recovery sales

Titled with a recovery certificate number, such as "RC No. 6/2023", and the tribunal's name, such as Debts Recovery Tribunal Pune. Read the sale proclamation itself: deposit, bid increments and payment deadlines are set by the tribunal process, not the bank's usual notice.

### Liquidation auctions

When a company is in liquidation, its liquidator sells assets, sometimes a factory, sometimes plant and machinery, sometimes the whole business. These listings show the liquidator's name rather than a bank. Reserve prices are often higher, and the terms (for example, whether you buy the company as a going concern or only the assets) need careful reading, ideally with professional advice.

## Reading a listing

Each listing on the register shows:

- **Property type and size**: flat, 1/2/3 BHK, plot, individual house, shop, office, industrial premises, industrial shed, or plant and machinery. Sizes appear in sq feet, sq metre or acres, so convert to one unit before comparing.
- **Location and PIN code**: the description often includes the society or project name, for example a flat in a named residency in Nagpur or Pune.
- **Bank or seller, and district**: the branch district is usually where the property is, but check the full notice.
- **Reserve price**: the minimum bid, not a valuation.
- **Auction date and status**: "Upcoming" means the auction has not yet happened.

A practical note: the same property can appear more than once, for example identical Bandra flat listings with the same size, price and date. Treat these as one property, and check whether the bank has published separate notices or re-listed the same one.

## The "as is where is" rule

Banks sell on an **as is where is, as is what is** basis. The buyer carries the risk of unpaid dues, encumbrances, occupants and litigation. In Maharashtra, check in particular:

1. **The full sale notice**: reserve price, earnest money deposit (EMD), bid increment, inspection date and payment schedule.
2. **Inspection**: visit on the inspection date. Many flats sold this way are in housing societies; ask whether the flat is vacant.
3. **Society dues and share certificate**: for flats in co-operative housing societies, ask the society about maintenance arrears and whether it will transfer membership.
4. **Land record**: for plots and houses, check the 7/12 extract (Satbara) and mutation entries. Our [Maharashtra property records guide](maharashtra-property-records.html) explains the record names, and the [Maharashtra registry](https://indiapropertyregister.com/ipregistry/maharashtra) lists districts, talukas and villages.
5. **RERA**: if the flat is in a recent project, look it up on the register's [RERA projects page](https://indiapropertyregister.com/rera).
6. **Court cases**: search for cases against the borrower, the company in liquidation or the property, including DRT matters.
7. **Costs beyond the bid**: stamp duty and registration on the sale certificate, plus any dues the notice says the buyer must clear.

## FAQ

**How many bank auction properties are listed in Maharashtra?**
15,689 listings on India Property Register as of October 2026, the second highest of any state after Haryana.

**What is a liquidation auction?**
A sale of a company's assets by its liquidator during liquidation. On the register these appear as "Liquidation auction: [company name]" with the liquidator's name as the seller.

**Is a bank auction property safe to buy?**
The bank sells it "as is where is", so the buyer takes the risk of dues, encumbrances and litigation. Read the sale notice, inspect the property, check the title and land record, and search court cases before bidding.
