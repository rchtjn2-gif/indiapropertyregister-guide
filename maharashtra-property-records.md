# Maharashtra property records: 7/12, 8A, property cards and flats explained

Maharashtra keeps property information in several different records depending on what the property is: farm land in a village, a house or shop in a city, or a flat in a housing society. Buyers often check the wrong one. This guide explains which record covers what, and how to find it.

## How big is the register?

India Property Register's Maharashtra registry holds over **1.23 crore farm and land plots** from the land records, **52.5 lakh city properties** from municipal tax records, **8.49 lakh flats** and **28,360 housing societies**, organised across all 36 districts. Pune, Thane, Nagpur and Nashik have the largest numbers of city properties and flats.

## Farm and village land: 7/12 and 8A

**7/12 extract (Satbara Utara).** The best-known Maharashtra land record. It combines two village forms: Form VII (the record of rights, showing holders and their rights) and Form XII (the crop record). It shows the survey or gat number, area, current holders, tenancy and other rights, encumbrances such as bank charges, and crops grown.

**8A extract.** The holding register: it lists all land held by one account holder in a village, useful to see a seller's total holding.

**Ferfar (mutation) entries.** Every change on the 7/12, such as a sale, inheritance or loan, is entered as a mutation with its own number. Reading the mutation history shows how the current holder got the land.

## City property: property cards and tax records

In surveyed urban areas, a **property card** records the city survey number, area and holder. Municipal corporations also keep **property tax records** for houses, shops and buildings, which show the assessed owner and tax details. A tax record is useful evidence of possession but is not, by itself, proof of title.

## Flats and housing societies

For a flat, the key documents are usually the registered agreement for sale or sale deed, the society's share certificate, and the building's approvals. For new projects, check the project's **MahaRERA registration**. The register lists housing societies and RERA projects alongside individual flats.

## Record of rights is not title

A 7/12 or property card shows who the government records as holder. It does not guarantee clean title. A proper title check traces registered deeds over many years, confirms mutations match those deeds, and searches for court cases and encumbrances. For any significant purchase, take a certified copy and have an advocate review it.

## A quick buyer checklist

1. Match the survey or gat number on the 7/12 with the sale documents.
2. Read the "other rights" column for loans, tenancy or restrictions.
3. Review recent mutation entries and whether any are pending.
4. For city property, compare the property card and tax record names.
5. For flats, check society membership and MahaRERA registration.
6. Check for pending court cases involving the seller or the property.

## Look up a property

Open the [Maharashtra property registry](https://indiapropertyregister.com/ipregistry/maharashtra) on India Property Register, pick a district, then a village or locality, then the property. For questions in plain words, try [Ask the register](https://indiapropertyregister.com/ask).
