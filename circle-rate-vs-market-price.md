# Circle rate vs market price: what buyers and sellers should know

When you register a property in India, two prices matter: the price you agree with the seller, and the government's minimum value for that property, the **circle rate**.

## What circle rates are

State governments notify a minimum value per unit area for land and buildings in each area. The name changes by state:

- **Circle rate**: Delhi, Uttar Pradesh and much of north India
- **Guideline value**: Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and others
- **Jantri**: Gujarat
- **Ready reckoner rate**: Maharashtra

Rates are revised from time to time and can differ by locality, road frontage, land use and type of construction.

## How they affect stamp duty and tax

- **Stamp duty and registration fees** are generally charged on the higher of the agreement value and the circle-rate value.
- **Income tax**: if a property is sold below the stamp-duty value by more than the permitted tolerance, the Income Tax Act can treat the stamp-duty value as the sale price for the seller, and the difference as income for the buyer (sections 50C and 56(2)(x)).

So a deal far below the circle rate can cost both sides more than expected.

## Why rates differ by land use

Agricultural, residential, commercial and industrial land in the same village can carry very different rates. A change of land use can change the applicable rate, so check what the land is recorded as.

## Looking up the rate

You can [ask India Property Register](https://indiapropertyregister.com/ask) about circle rates for a village or locality; it shows the source and date of each fact. Always confirm the current notified rate with the state's registration department before registering.

## Questions to ask before you register

1. What is the current circle rate for this exact locality and land use?
2. Is the agreed price above or below it?
3. What stamp duty and registration fee apply, and who pays them?
4. Are there any concessions (for example for women buyers in some states)?

Information only. For tax questions, consult a chartered accountant.
