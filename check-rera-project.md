# Check a RERA project before you book a flat

The **Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA)** changed how new housing is sold in India. Before a project can be advertised or sold, it must be registered with the state's Real Estate Regulatory Authority, and the registration number must appear in every advertisement. That gives buyers something concrete to check. This page explains how to use it, and where its limits are.

## Which projects must register

Under RERA, a project on land above **500 square metres** or with more than **8 units** must be registered with the state RERA before advertising or sale. Real estate agents who sell such projects must register too.

## How many projects are registered

India Property Register collects state RERA registrations in one place. Its [RERA page](https://indiapropertyregister.com/rera) lists **1,68,513 projects** and **61,370 agents** across 23 states (October 2026). Maharashtra has by far the most, with 50,017 projects, followed by Tamil Nadu (37,117), Gujarat (17,634) and Telangana (11,306).

## Step 1: Find the registration number

Look for it on the brochure, hoarding, website or advertisement. If a project is being sold without a number, ask why. In Maharashtra, project numbers typically begin with **P5** followed by digits (for example P518... for Mumbai Suburban or P521... for Pune), and you can browse every [Maharashtra RERA project](https://indiapropertyregister.com/rera/maharashtra) with its promoter and location.

## Step 2: Match the details

Check that the registration shows:

- the **same project name** and phase or wing you are being offered (many projects register wings separately),
- the **same promoter** company named in your agreement,
- the **location** you visited,
- the **status** and the **proposed completion date**.

A different wing or phase may have a different registration and a different completion date.

## Step 3: Read the promoter's filings on the state RERA site

State RERA portals hold the promoter's declarations, approvals, project updates and complaints. Look at quarterly progress updates and whether the completion date has been extended.

## What RERA registration does not prove

**Registration is not a guarantee of clear title.** The promoter files a title declaration with RERA, but the authority does not certify the title. Separately check:

- the **land record** for the plot the project stands on,
- any **encumbrance** (mortgages or charges) on the land,
- whether the land is involved in **litigation**,
- the **sanctioned plan** against what is being built.

## Step 4: Check the agent

If you are buying through a broker, confirm the agent's own RERA registration as well.

## Red flags

- No registration number on marketing material
- Registration in a different promoter's name
- Completion date already extended more than once
- Pressure to pay a large booking amount before an agreement for sale

Taking an hour for these checks before paying anything is the cheapest protection a buyer has.
