# Uttar Pradesh property records: khatauni, khasra and city tax records explained

Uttar Pradesh has more land records than almost any other state: India Property Register's UP registry holds over **1.57 crore farm and land plots**, **57.9 lakh city properties**, **1.05 lakh flats** and **1,752 housing societies**, across all 75 districts. Each kind of property sits in a different record, so it pays to know which one you are looking at.

## Farm and village land

**Khatauni.** The record of rights for a village. It is organised by **khata** (account), and lists the account holders, their shares, and the plots (gata or khasra numbers) in that account. It is the document most people mean when they talk about UP land records, and it is what the state's Bhulekh portal shows.

**Khasra.** The field book, plot by plot: area, land class, crops and possession. Where the khatauni tells you *who* holds an account, the khasra tells you about *each plot*.

**Khata number vs gata number.** A khata number is the holder's account. A gata (plot) number identifies a parcel of land. One khata can contain many gatas, and one gata can be shared across several holders.

**Mutation (dakhil-kharij).** After a sale or inheritance, the new holder's name is entered in the khatauni through mutation. Until mutation is done, the record still shows the old holder.

## City property

In towns and cities, houses, shops and buildings are recorded in **municipal property tax records**, which show the assessed owner and tax details. Districts such as Lucknow, Kanpur Nagar, Ghaziabad and Agra have large numbers of city properties on the register. As in other states, a tax record supports possession but is not proof of title on its own.

## Flats and development authorities

Flats in Ghaziabad, Gautam Buddha Nagar (Noida and Greater Noida), Lucknow and other cities are often in projects registered with **UP RERA**, and many plots are allotted by development authorities. Before buying, check the project's RERA registration, the builder's approvals and whether the flat is free of loans.

## Record of rights is not title

The khatauni records who the government recognises as holder. Title comes from registered deeds. A careful buyer checks:

1. The khatauni entries for the exact gata numbers being sold.
2. Whether all co-holders on the khata are part of the sale.
3. Recent and pending mutations.
4. Land class: agricultural land may need conversion before construction.
5. Loans or charges noted on the record.
6. Court cases involving the property or the sellers.

For any significant purchase, get certified copies and an advocate's title report.

## Look up a property

Start at the [Uttar Pradesh property registry](https://indiapropertyregister.com/ipregistry/uttar-pradesh) on India Property Register, choose a district, then a village or locality. To see which states and record types are covered, check [coverage by state](https://indiapropertyregister.com/coverage).
